Door 03 · the deployer

Deploy mine.

Four things, then wait. The deployer logs into your server over SSH, changes the root password to one you choose, installs Megamesh exactly as the public script would, and comes back with your admin login. Nothing you type is stored anywhere — it lives in this tab and in the requests that carry it.

Your server

A fresh VPS with CentOS Stream 9 or Ubuntu 24.04 (both verified end to end, including through this page) — Rocky/Alma 9 and Debian 12 may work but are not yet tested. 1 vCPU and 1 GB of RAM are enough. You need root over SSH with a password, and a domain whose DNS you can edit.

New root password · recommended

The one your provider sent has already travelled by email. The deployer replaces it on the server before installing anything, and uses the new one from then on. Save it in your password manager now; it is shown again at hand-over.

Read before you click. Your root password is sent over HTTPS to megamesh.sh, used to log into your server for the duration of the install, and discarded. Each status refresh logs in again with it. We keep no copy and no logs of it. If you would rather not, the script gives the same result without us in the middle.

Done. Your VPN is installed.

Three things left, all on your side. Save the credentials first — they are shown once.

  1. Point your domain at the server. At your DNS provider (Namecheap, Cloudflare, OVH…), create an A record: host @, value . Remove any parking or redirect record on the same name. Caddy on your server is already waiting and will fetch a certificate by itself once the record resolves — usually within minutes.
  2. Log in. Open (type the https://), sign in with the admin credentials above, then enable two-factor authentication under your account. Add a device, scan the QR code, connect.
  3. Have a techie friend finish the hardening — twenty minutes with the hardening notes: install an SSH key and turn passwords off, optionally make the admin page VPN-only. Everything else (firewall, fail2ban, automatic updates, weekly health-checked upgrades) is already running.
