01 / Why
subscriber → operator

Renting a tunnel, you're a subscriber. Running one, you're the operator. Commercial VPNs were built for scale, not for you: a shared exit, a privacy policy you take on faith, a box you will never see. Most self-hosting guides stop at “WireGuard is up” and leave the door open. Megamesh is the rest of the job — hardened by default, on a VPS that costs less than the subscription.

03 / What you get

Three doors.
Everything else is a wall.

The public internet reaches exactly three ports. The firewall's default is DROP, not reject: scanners get silence. Inside, two containers and a handful of host settings — all written down, none of them clever.

Firewall

Three doors, one wall

tcp/22, udp/51820, tcp/443 and nothing else. No port 80, no admin port, no Docker ports leaking through. ICMP limited to ping and path-MTU.

SSH

Key-only, then fail2ban

Password login stays on until a key is proven to work, then goes away. Three tries, 24 h bans doubling to 30 days. 76,073 attempts became a handful of addresses.

TLS

Automatic certificates

Caddy terminates 443 with Let's Encrypt over TLS-ALPN — no port 80 ever opens. HSTS, strict headers, Server header stripped.

Admin

wg-easy, behind the wall

Peers, QR codes, stats, 2FA. The UI port is never published; the admin account is created unattended before 443 is reachable, so nobody can race you to the wizard.

Upgrades

Weekly, health-checked

topgrade pulls packages and container images, restarts, verifies the tunnel is up and the admin answers — or rolls back to the images that worked. Pinned by SHA-256.

VPN-only mode

Admin reachable only from inside

One command makes the admin page answer only through the tunnel; outsiders get a 302 to any URL you choose. Certificates keep renewing.

05 / Honest notes

What this is not.

A personal VPN changes who can see your traffic, not whether anyone can. The repository says exactly what has and has not been exercised.

— 01

Not a privacy service

Your VPS provider sees your traffic leave their network. Megamesh hides you from the café Wi-Fi and your ISP, not from a subpoena.

— 02

Not multi-tenant

One admin, your devices. wg-easy supports many peers, but the hardening assumes a single trusted operator.

— 03

Not finished by the deployer

It changes your root password but leaves SSH on a password until someone installs a key, and leaves 2FA and VPN-only mode for you to switch on. Twenty minutes with the hardening notes finishes it.

— 04

Not tested everywhere

CentOS Stream 9 and Ubuntu 24.04 on a 1 vCPU / 1 GB VPS are verified end to end — the second one through the deployer itself. Rocky, Alma and Debian share those code paths but have not been run yet.

06 / Your exit

One address.
Yours.

Once your devices are on the tunnel, everything you do leaves from your server's address. That is the point — and a fact others can use: a friend can allow-list you, your own boxes can trust you, a rate limit can make an exception. The author publishes his, and the repository is the source of truth for it.

Publishing it is also a defence. Calumny, fake news, a “someone broke into our app” accusation — every action on the internet leaves an address in a log. Declare yours in public, with a start date, and any trace that comes from somewhere else is, by your own prior declaration, not you. It does not prove innocence by itself, but it gives you leverage: a public, dated, verifiable statement made before anyone had a reason to doubt it.
